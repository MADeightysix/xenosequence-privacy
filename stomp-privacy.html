STOMP!

Privacy Notice

Effective and last updated: November 10, 2026

STOMP! is a Twitch chat tournament game operated by Mitchell Depriest (MADeightysix). This notice covers the STOMP Twitch Extension, broadcaster desk, game backend, and stream overlays.

Information we process

  • Twitch identity: Twitch user and channel IDs, display names, and public profile-picture URLs. Viewers share their Twitch identity through the Extension to submit authenticated choices and receive cosmetics. Joining through the panel, video component, or a channel’s chat command lets the broadcaster register your Twitch profile in that channel’s game.
  • Game activity: join commands, queues, team assignments, animal choices and lineups, attack counts, readiness, match results, and cosmetic selections. The backend holds secret choices so it can resolve matches.
  • Cosmetic transactions: Twitch transaction ID, user and channel IDs, product identifier, Bits amount, test/development status, delivery status, and timestamp. Twitch handles Bits purchases; STOMP does not receive payment-card, banking, or tax information from players.
  • Authentication: broadcaster access and refresh tokens and viewer Extension authorization tokens. These are handled in memory rather than saved to STOMP’s database or browser storage. The server briefly caches a hash of a validated broadcaster token and identity details.
  • Service delivery: hosting and network providers receive connection information such as IP addresses and browser/request details when delivering the site and its resources. If you email support, we receive the information you send.

How we use information

We use this information to authenticate players and broadcasters, recognize join commands, run and display matches, deliver and remember cosmetics, prevent duplicate transaction grants and unauthorized actions, and respond to support and privacy requests. STOMP reads channel chat in the broadcaster’s browser to recognize join commands; it does not maintain a general chat-message archive.

What other people can see

Your display name, profile picture, participation, team, revealed animals, results, and equipped cosmetics may appear to other players, in the Twitch Extension, and on the broadcaster’s stream or recordings. Choices awaiting reveal are withheld from public game responses. Onslaught army totals and composition are game information. Broadcasters may broadcast, record, or clip the displayed game; deleting STOMP records does not remove copies controlled by Twitch, broadcasters, or viewers.

Service providers and sharing

STOMP uses Twitch for identity, profiles, chat, Extension functionality, and Bits verification, and OpenAI Sites and Cloudflare infrastructure to host the service and store game records. Twitch profile pictures load from Twitch’s CDN. The broadcaster desk loads Google Fonts; the packaged Extension uses system fonts. These providers receive information necessary to deliver their services and may process it under their own policies.

STOMP does not sell personal information or use game data for targeted advertising. We may disclose information when required by law or when reasonably necessary to protect the service and its users. Service providers may process information in countries other than your own.

Browser storage

The Extension stores an unresolved cosmetic transaction ID in browser local storage, scoped to the channel and user, so a failed delivery can be retried. You can clear this through your browser’s site-data controls, although that may remove the pending retry reference. The local demo uses temporary in-memory state. STOMP does not add advertising or analytics tracking scripts; Twitch and hosting services may use their own storage or cookies.

Retention

Channel game state and cosmetic entitlements are persistent. Recent Standard-mode revealed results are limited to 20 entries in the active room state. Standard ballots are cleared after a reveal; other mode lineups and battle details can remain in the current game state until it is replaced or reset. Broadcaster session tokens are discarded when the desk session ends or signs out.

Drop Ship stores your linked Twitch user ID, chosen animal, drop count, display name, avatar URL, and last drop time in the current round to enforce one species per viewer. The overlay shows animal totals and positions and displays the winning viewers’ Twitch names, avatars, and drop counts at the end of a round. Drop Ship replaces its round records when the cooldown ends and the game next updates, or when the broadcaster turns it off.

Persistent game, cosmetic, and transaction records currently have no automatic expiry. They remain until manually removed or replaced. Transaction records support purchase delivery, duplicate-grant prevention, and dispute handling. We review deletion requests manually and may retain limited records where necessary for legal obligations, security, or unresolved transaction issues. This notice does not promise a fixed automatic deletion period.

Your choices and privacy requests

You can decline identity sharing, stop participating, and revoke STOMP’s access through Twitch Connections settings. Revoking access stops future authorization; it does not automatically erase existing records. Broadcasters can sign out of the desk and deactivate the Extension.

To request access, correction, or deletion of STOMP-held information, email the contact below with your Twitch username or user ID and the relevant channel. We may ask you to verify account control. Do not send passwords, access tokens, or payment details. Deletion can remove game history and cosmetic entitlements. Depending on where you live, you may have additional data-protection rights or a right to contact a local supervisory authority.

Security and age requirements

STOMP uses HTTPS and authorization checks to protect backend access and private choices. No online service can guarantee complete security. Keep broadcaster tokens and overlay access URLs private. STOMP is intended for people eligible to use Twitch and is not directed to children under 13 or below the applicable minimum age. Contact us if you believe a child’s information has been provided inappropriately.

Changes and other policies

We will update this page and its revision date when practices change. Twitch’s own data handling is described in the Twitch Privacy Notice. This notice describes STOMP’s practices and does not replace Twitch’s policies.
